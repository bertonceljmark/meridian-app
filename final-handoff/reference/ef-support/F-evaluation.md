# F. Critical evaluation

18 Oct 2026. My own judgement of this workstream. Nothing is approved.

## The central question

*Could Meridian help Mark remember, choose, initiate and follow through at the moments he would otherwise struggle, without creating another thing to manage?*

**Partly, and more on remembering and starting than on choosing.** The best evidence points to two concrete gaps:
- Turning a vague intention into a plan with a "when". Planning is the documented deficit.
- Cueing it at an event rather than a clock time. Event-based remembering is largely intact in adult ADHD.

Both are cheap to support and work for any activity. Holding it reliably, then making the first ten minutes easy, is a plausible second step.

Choosing among possibilities and opportunistic suggestions have weak evidence and the highest risk of becoming noise. Nothing here has been shown to reduce functional impairment in adults with ADHD. It is support, not treatment.

## Strong evidence (as strong as this field gets)

- **Planning and time-based cueing are the documented deficits** (A, small samples). Event-based anchors and one-line if-then capture target them directly.
- **Reminders help one-off, time-bound actions** (B). This supports deadline windows.
- **Prompt effects fade within weeks, and smart timing adds little** (B). This supports user-named moments, a hard interruption cap and persistence without repetition.
- **Expected rewards reduce voluntary engagement** (B, contested). This supports never scoring held items, ideas or gestures.

## Speculative (C), worth testing on Mark

- **Next-moment deferral** ("Later" goes to the next moment of the same kind instead of a snooze interval).
- **The first step plus a bounded runway**, and using the same runway as a stop point for hyperfocus.
- **The Cross transition.** Detachment research is correlational, and the plan-making result is unreplicated.
- **Ambient visibility** keeping intentions alive without re-notifying.
- **Rare, varied ideas** staying welcome rather than becoming background noise.

## What could become annoying

- **Cross.** It is a daily offer, even when silent. It's the most likely to be ignored and then resented. Cut it first if the self-test is lukewarm.
- **Last calls for deadlines already done but not marked.** That is one unnecessary interruption a week, the most likely nag.
- **The Held list growing.** Past about 15 items it becomes a backlog with a nicer name, which is exactly what Mark said he won't maintain.
- **Ideas about his girlfriend.** Even with good wording, being reminded to be thoughtful can feel managed. Ideas should come from Mark.
- **The "started this month" counts on the Start screen.** They may read as a second scoreboard. I'd test without them.
- **Permissions.** Calendar, location, a Focus filter and AlarmKit are each reasonable, but asking for them together would feel like setting up a system. Ask for each only when Mark picks a moment that needs it.

## Rejected

- **Inferred receptivity or "smart" timing.** It is wrong 20–30% of the time at best, its gains decay, and there is no ADHD evidence.
- **A morning card as the organising surface.**
- **A weekly planning ritual.**
- **LLM-generated suggestions.**
- **Temptation bundling, body doubling, accountability partners.**
- **Reframing prompts.**
- **Brain training.**
- **Any score for focus, productivity or follow-through.**
- **Rewarding the choosing or holding of an intention.**

## Risks to Meridian itself

- **Drift into a task manager.** Hold is the strongest idea and also the one most likely to pull Meridian towards being a to-do app. The guardrails are no priorities, no due dates on intentions, items that fade instead of going overdue, and a soft limit. They still need Mark's decision on one-off intentions (E).
- **Letting this become the new organising principle.** This is the pattern the project has fallen into before. The integration in E deliberately adds no tab and no daily surface.
- **Dependence.** If Meridian becomes Mark's memory, a silent failure (a stale calendar cue, a Focus filter that didn't run) costs more than having no system. Delivery must be redundant, and failures should be visible.

## Recommended next step

If Mark wants to continue: carry **Hold** (with deadline windows and ambient persistence) and **Start** into the main design thread as candidate capabilities, and run a 2–3 week self-test before building anything further.

Cross and the ideas shelf stay in research until the self-test shows they earn a place.
