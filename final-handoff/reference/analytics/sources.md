# Sources (verified 18 Oct 2026)

Compiled for Workstream B. Each source was fetched and checked, mostly at abstract level (noted per entry). The "*App:*" lines are a research assistant's first-pass implications, **not decisions**. Where one conflicts with Mark's rules (for example, asking why a substance was taken, a daily check-in, or a "stressful today?" tag), B and C do not adopt it.


## 1. Case-only designs (case-crossover, SCCS) and migraine triggers
- **Maclure M.** The case-crossover design: a method for studying transient effects on the risk of acute events. *Am J Epidemiol* 1991;133(2):144-53. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1985444/ . Proposes a design that uses only cases: each case's exposure just before onset is compared with its own earlier periods. Self-matching removes control-selection bias and fixed confounders, and the effect-window length is something you have to choose. *App:* compare the hours or days before each headache with the same person's control windows. The window length counts as a researcher choice (see 6).
- **Whitaker HJ, Farrington CP, Spiessens B, Musonda P.** Tutorial in biostatistics: the self-controlled case series method. *Stat Med* 2006;25(10):1768-97. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16220518/ . SCCS uses only people who had the event and compares within each person, so fixed covariates are controlled automatically. The tutorial covers the method's assumptions and limitations. *App:* with one user, this is an event-rate ratio for "risk windows after an exposure" vs the rest of the time. Its assumptions (for example, that the event does not change later exposure) need checking.
- **Houle TT, Turner DP.** Natural experimentation is a challenging method for identifying headache triggers. *Headache* 2013;53(4):636-43. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23534852/ (PMC5421357). Weather, stress and hormones vary so much from day to day that two "similar" days to compare are rare: a median of 2.3 days/year for weather, and about 1.5–2 days/month for stress or hormones. Sound conclusions need formal experiments or statistical balancing. *App:* naive "days with X vs without X" comparisons are fragile. Adjust for context and present results as hypotheses.
- **Turner DP, Smitherman TA, Martin VT, Penzien DB, Houle TT.** Causality and headache triggers. *Headache* 2013;53(4):628-35. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23534872/ . Applies Rubin's causal model and concludes that the assumptions needed to call something a trigger are unlikely to hold under natural experimentation. It recommends formal designs or diary studies analysed with advanced statistical models. *App:* supports the app's "no causal claims" stance and suggests offering a self-experiment as a next step.
- **Mostofsky E, Mittleman MA, Buettner C, Li W, Bertisch SM.** Prospective cohort study of caffeinated beverage intake as a potential trigger of headaches among migraineurs. *Am J Med* 2019;132(8):984-991. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31402050/ (PMC6744320). 98 adults kept twice-daily e-diaries. Each person's migraine incidence on intake days was compared with their own no-intake days, adjusting for day of week (conditional logistic regression). The association was nonlinear: only high intake was linked to migraine. *App:* a worked example of self-matched diary analysis. Allow dose-response shapes and day-of-week effects.

## 2. Confounding by indication / reverse causation
- **Salas M, Hofman A, Stricker BH.** Confounding by indication: an example of variation in the use of epidemiologic terminology. *Am J Epidemiol* 1999;149(11):981-3. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10355372/ . Defines confounding by indication and separates it from protopathic bias (the drug is taken because of early symptoms of the outcome), confounding by severity, and selection bias. *App:* "melatonin on bad-sleep nights" or "painkiller then headache" is protopathic bias. Flag items the user takes in response to symptoms.
- **Signorello LB, McLaughlin JK, Lipworth L, Friis S, Sørensen HT, Blot WJ.** Confounding by indication in epidemiologic studies of commonly used analgesics. *Am J Ther* 2002;9(3):199-205. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11941379/ . Reviews examples where the reason for taking even common over-the-counter analgesics biased drug–outcome associations. *App:* do not report drug-then-symptom links at face value. Ask the user why they took the substance, or record the reason.

## 3. Missingness and reporting schedules in EMA / self-tracking
- **Shiffman S, Stone AA, Hufford MR.** Ecological momentary assessment. *Annu Rev Clin Psychol* 2008;4:1-32. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18509902/ . A review of real-time sampling to reduce recall bias. It separates designs that record particular events (event-contingent) from designs that prompt at periodic or random times (time- or signal-contingent). *App:* user-logged headaches are event-contingent: a day with no entry might be a day with no headache or a day nobody logged. A light daily check-in helps tell these apart.
- **Stone AA, Shiffman S, Schwartz JE, Broderick JE, Hufford MR.** Patient compliance with paper and electronic diaries. *Control Clin Trials* 2003;24(2):182-99. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12689739/ . Paper diaries reported 90% compliance, but actual compliance was 11% (backfilling). Time-stamped e-diaries reached 94%. *App:* keep the entry timestamp separate from the event time, and treat backfilled entries as less reliable.
- **Rintala A, Wampers M, Myin-Germeys I, Viechtbauer W.** Response compliance and predictors thereof in studies using the experience sampling method. *Psychol Assess* 2019;31(2):226-235. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30394762/ . Pooled ESM data (n=1,717): compliance fell across days and varied by time of day (56% early morning vs 83% midday) and by clinical group. *App:* missingness has structure, so model it or show it rather than assuming random gaps. (None of these sources tests MNAR in personal self-tracking directly; that is our inference.)

## 4. N-of-1 / single-case time-series analysis
- **Bernal JL, Cummins S, Gasparrini A.** Interrupted time series regression for the evaluation of public health interventions: a tutorial. *Int J Epidemiol* 2017;46(1):348-355. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27283160/ (PMC5407170). Covers segmented regression, specifying the impact model in advance, autocorrelation, seasonality, over-dispersion and time-varying confounders. *App:* to analyse "started routine X on date D", fit level and slope changes, handle day-of-week and autocorrelation, and fix the expected shape of the effect before looking.
- **Daza EJ.** Causal analysis of self-tracked time series data using a counterfactual framework for N-of-1 trials. *Methods Inf Med* 2018;57(1):e10-e21. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29621835/ (PMC6087468). Frames n-of-1 observational associations as hypotheses to test later in n-of-1 trials. Covers autocorrelation, trends, carryover and slow onset/decay. *App:* label findings "candidate" and offer a structured self-test.
- **Percha B, Baskerville EB, Johnson M, Dudley JT, Zimmerman N.** Designing robust N-of-1 studies for precision medicine: simulation study and design recommendations. *J Med Internet Res* 2019;21(4):e12641. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30932871/ (PMC6462889). Simulation shows that the number of blocks, treatment duration, ordering, carryover/wash-in and sampling frequency drive the ability to detect real effects. *App:* set minimum-data thresholds by simulation instead of a fixed "N days" rule.

## 5. Sensitivity to unmeasured confounding
- **VanderWeele TJ, Ding P.** Sensitivity analysis in observational research: introducing the E-value. *Ann Intern Med* 2017;167(4):268-274. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28693043/ . The E-value is the minimum risk-ratio strength that an unmeasured confounder would need with both exposure and outcome to explain away the association. Report it for the estimate and for the CI limit nearest the null. *App:* gives a plain-language robustness label, e.g. "a hidden factor would need to roughly double both to explain this".
- **Lipsitch M, Tchetgen Tchetgen E, Cohen T.** Negative controls: a tool for detecting confounding and bias in observational studies. *Epidemiology* 2010;21(3):383-8. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20335814/ (PMC3053408). Negative-control exposures and outcomes (ones that should show no effect) can reveal confounding, recall bias or analytic flaws. *App:* run the same pipeline on implausible pairs, such as a future exposure or an unrelated item. If those also look "significant", the finding is suspect.

## 6. Multiple comparisons / forking paths
- **Benjamini Y, Hochberg Y.** Controlling the false discovery rate: a practical and powerful approach to multiple testing. *J R Stat Soc B* 1995;57(1):289-300. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2517-6161.1995.tb02031.x (Crossref summary). Defines the FDR and a step-up procedure that controls it for independent tests, with more power than FWER control. *App:* scanning many item pairs needs FDR control. The proof assumes independence, and lagged and overlapping tests are correlated.
- **Gelman A, Hill J, Yajima M.** Why we (usually) don't have to worry about multiple comparisons. *J Res Educ Eff* 2012;5(2):189-211. https://doi.org/10.1080/19345747.2011.618213 (preprint arXiv:0907.2478). Multilevel models partially pool estimates toward each other instead of widening intervals, which addresses multiple comparisons. *App:* shrink all candidate effects together with a hierarchical model, so weakly supported pairs fall back toward zero.
- **Gelman A, Loken E.** The garden of forking paths (working paper, 14 Nov 2013). https://sites.stat.columbia.edu/gelman/research/unpublished/p_hacking.pdf (full text read). Data-contingent analysis choices create a multiple-comparisons problem even when only one analysis is run. *App:* lags, windows and thresholds count as tests. Fix them in advance or include them in the correction.

## 7. Daily stressors, spillover, adaptation to life events
- **Bolger N, DeLongis A, Kessler RC, Schilling EA.** Effects of daily stress on negative mood. *J Pers Soc Psychol* 1989;57(5):808-18. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2810026/ . 166 couples kept 6-week diaries. Daily stressors explained up to 20% of within-person mood variance, and interpersonal conflict was the most distressing. Habituation set in by day 2 for most repeated stressors, multiple stressors on one day plateaued rather than adding up, and mood the next day was better than it would otherwise have been. *App:* model a life event as a short window with nonlinear, non-additive effects, not a constant offset, and expect a rebound.
- **Almeida DM, Wethington E, Kessler RC.** The Daily Inventory of Stressful Events: an interview-based approach for measuring daily stressors. *Assessment* 2002;9(1):41-55. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11911234/ . In the NSDE national sample (n=1,031), at least one stressor occurred on 40% of days. Interpersonal tensions and network stressors predicted symptoms and mood. *App:* expect a stressor on most of a user's analysis windows. A quick "anything stressful today? (type)" tag is a validated-style covariate.
- **Luhmann M, Hofmann W, Eid M, Lucas RE.** Subjective well-being and adaptation to life events: a meta-analysis. *J Pers Soc Psychol* 2012;102(3):592-615. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22059843/ (PMC3289759). 313 samples: reactions and adaptation differ by event and by affective vs cognitive well-being, and do not depend on how desirable the event is. *App:* major events need their own decay profiles, with no single default half-life. Mask or flag the period around a major event.

## 8. Melatonin and Apple Watch sleep
- **Ferracioli-Oda E, Qawasmi A, Bloch MH.** Meta-analysis: melatonin for the treatment of primary sleep disorders. *PLoS One* 2013;8(5):e63773. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23691095/ (PMC3656905). Across 19 RCTs (n=1,683), sleep latency fell by 7.06 min (95% CI 4.37–9.75), total sleep time rose by 8.25 min, and quality improved by SMD 0.22. The benefits are modest. *App:* a true effect of about 7 min is small next to night-to-night noise, so one person's data will rarely detect it. Say so.
- **Robbins R, Weaver MD, Sullivan JP, et al.** Accuracy of three commercial wearable devices for sleep tracking in healthy adults. *Sensors* 2024;24(20):6532. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39460013/ (PMC11511193). One night of PSG in 35 healthy adults. Apple Watch Series 8 detected sleep vs wake with sensitivity of 95% or more, but underestimated wake by 7 min and deep sleep by 43 min and overestimated light sleep by 45 min. Total sleep duration was similar to PSG. *App:* trust duration more than stage minutes. The study population had no sleep disorders.
- **Apple Developer: HKCategoryValueSleepAnalysis.** https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkcategoryvaluesleepanalysis . Says that awake samples recorded by Apple Watch occur only between two sleep samples. Apps derive time-to-fall-asleep by comparing an inBed sample with the sleep samples. *App:* the Watch alone gives no sleep-onset latency. Latency needs an in-bed time (from the user or another source), and subjective quality must be self-reported.

## 9. Communicating uncertainty
- **Hullman J, Resnick P, Adar E.** Hypothetical outcome plots outperform error bars and violin plots for inferences about reliability of variable ordering. *PLoS One* 2015;10(11):e0142444. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0142444 (PMC4646698). Animated individual draws (HOPs) need little statistical background, and in an experiment people judged more accurately with HOPs than with error bars or violins. *App:* show a few plausible outcomes rather than a CI bar.
- **Kay M, Kola T, Hullman J, Munson SA.** When (ish) is my bus? User-centered visualizations of uncertainty in everyday, mobile predictive systems. *Proc CHI 2016*:5092-5103. https://doi.org/10.1145/2858036.2858558 (abstract via OpenAlex). Introduces quantile dotplots for small screens. They reduced the variance of laypeople's probability estimates by about 1.15× vs density plots and increased confidence. *App:* a strong fit for phone UI, e.g. "in 20 similar weeks, 14 dots…".

## 10. Personal informatics practice and pitfalls
- **Epstein DA, Ping A, Fogarty J, Munson SA.** A lived informatics model of personal informatics. *Proc UbiComp 2015*:731-742. https://doi.org/10.1145/2750858.2804250 (PMC12435389). Based on surveys and 22 interviews, the model covers deciding to track, collection, integration, reflection, and lapsing and resuming. *App:* lapses are normal. Design for gaps and restarts, and do not read a gap as "no event".
- **Choe EK, Lee NB, Lee B, Pratt W, Kientz JA.** Understanding quantified-selfers' practices in collecting and exploring personal data. *Proc CHI 2014*:1143-1152. https://doi.org/10.1145/2556288.2557372 (abstract via OpenAlex). An analysis of 52 QS Meetup talks found common pitfalls: tracking too many things, not tracking triggers and context, and insufficient scientific rigor. *App:* nudge users toward a few focused items plus context tags, and build in rigor (corrections, controls).

## 11. Apple platform (developer.apple.com, doc pages read)
- **EventKit access levels.** https://developer.apple.com/documentation/eventkit/accessing-the-event-store ; `requestFullAccessToEvents(completion:)` and `requestWriteOnlyAccessToEvents(completion:)`, both iOS 17.0+ / macOS 14.0+. Write-only access lets an app create events but not read any events, including its own. Read-only access does not exist, so reading needs full access. *App:* using calendar events as life-event covariates requires the full-access prompt. Explain why it is needed and degrade gracefully if refused.
- **HealthKit headache symptom.** https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkcategorytypeidentifier/headache (iOS 13.6+). "A category type that records headache as a symptom", with values from `HKCategoryValueSeverity` (notPresent, mild, moderate, severe, unspecified). *App:* headaches can be read from and written to Health with severity, and "notPresent" allows explicit no-headache logs that help with missingness.
- **HealthKit State of Mind.** https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkstateofmind (iOS 18.0+ / watchOS 11.0+). The initializer takes date, kind, valence (Double), labels and associations, and the kinds are `dailyMood` and `momentaryEmotion`. *App:* mood can be imported as a standard outcome, with daily and momentary kept as separate series.
