# R2: Maintaining meaningful routines across well-being

Most figures come from abstracts or summaries; secondary-only sources are noted.

## 1. Established evidence

**Maintenance and lapses**

1. **Maintenance runs on outcomes already experienced; starting runs on expected outcomes.** Rothman proposed that people start a behaviour because of what they expect from it, and keep it up because they are satisfied with what it has actually given them. Studies of walking groups, cardiac rehab and smoking cessation found outcome satisfaction predicts maintenance. *Moderate.* https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/toward-a-theory-based-analysis-of-behavioral-maintenance/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3267380 · *Meridian:* the core loop should help Mark notice and remember what a routine gave him. Counting how often he did it isn't enough.
2. **Immediate rewards predict persistence; delayed ones don't.** Enjoyment during the activity predicted persistence with resolutions, studying and exercise. Long-term benefits did not, even though people said those were why they did it. *Moderate* (5 studies). https://www.chicagobooth.edu/review/achieve-long-term-goals-focus-short-term · *Meridian:* the in-the-moment experience matters more than later reports.
3. **Maintenance theory has five themes:** motives, self-regulation, habit, resources and social/environmental context (review of 100 theories). *Moderate.* https://storre.stir.ac.uk/handle/1893/25036 · Habit is one of the five.
4. **A single miss didn't change daily habit formation.** But the study was small (39 good fits out of 62 participants), time to plateau ranged from 18 to 254 days, and it has not been replicated. *Moderate/weak.* https://rr.peercommunityin.org/download/t_recommendations.reply_pdf.8382af6b73bdd0f7.6d616e757363726970745f7072657072696e742e706466.pdf
5. **For weekly behaviour, repetition count didn't predict automaticity; feeling good after the session did.** 226 students in weekly classes. *Weak/moderate* (observational). https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.578108/full · *Meridian:* habit-strength curves are a poor model for weekly or monthly routines.
6. **The abstinence-violation effect.** Blaming a lapse on oneself as a stable, global failing ("I always blow it"), plus guilt, turns a lapse into a relapse. Blaming a temporary, external cause makes recovery more likely. *Moderate as a framework* (the dynamic model is still unvalidated). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10890810/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6760427
7. **Self-compassion after failure raised motivation to improve.** Compared with a self-esteem boost or a control condition, it produced more study time after a failed test and more motivation to fix a weakness. *Moderate* (4 experiments). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22645164/ Self-compassion also correlates with health behaviours (r≈.25 across 15 samples, cross-sectional, one lab). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25243717/
8. **Broken streaks.** Showing an unbroken streak raises continuation, independent of actual past behaviour. Showing a broken one lowers it, more so when people blame themselves, and less so when the streak can be repaired. *Moderate* (7 studies, JCR 2023). https://udspace.udel.edu/handle/19716/34160 ; https://www.colorado.edu/business/news/2023/04/20/research-streaks-marketing-tech-barasch
9. **Built-in "emergency reserves" (planned slack) beat both easy and hard goals for persistence after a miss.** The mechanism is that more progress is perceived after a miss. *Moderate* (1 field and 4 lab studies). https://chibe.upenn.edu/publications/nudging-persistence-after-failure-through-emergency-reserves/
10. **Progress monitoring improves goal attainment** (d≈0.40, 138 RCTs), more so when progress is physically recorded or reported to others. *Strong.* https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/91437/
11. **Small-area effect.** Motivation rises when attention goes to whichever is smaller, the progress made or the distance left. *Moderate.* https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jconrs/doi10.1086-663827.html · *Meridian:* framing should move from "done so far" early in a period to "left to go" late in it.
12. **Measurement makes enjoyable activities feel like work.** People did more but enjoyed it less, and continued engagement and well-being fell. *Moderate* (6 experiments). https://marketing.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Etkin-Jordan-11-12-15-Hidden-Cost.pdf · *Meridian:* this is the main hazard for leisure, creative and relational routines.

**Low-frequency positive routines**

13. **Scheduling leisure for a specific time makes it feel like a chore and lowers enjoyment.** Scheduling "roughly" (an afternoon, not 3 pm) avoids this, and scheduling still raises the chance it happens. *Moderate* (13 studies, JMR 2016). https://source.wustl.edu/2016/03/scheduling-takes-fun-free-time/
14. **Anticipation carries much of the value.** Vacationers were happier before their trips; the after-effect was gone by about 8 weeks (N=1,530). https://repub.eur.nl/pub/19281/ Waiting for an experience is more pleasant than waiting for a possession (4 studies). https://news.cornell.edu/node/283533 *Moderate.*
15. **People underestimate how much they'll enjoy rediscovering ordinary moments later.** They are accurate for extraordinary ones (time-capsule studies). *Moderate.* https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/rediscovering-our-mundane-moments-brings-us-unexpected-pleasure.html Positive reminiscence raised happiness in one small study (N=65). *Weak.* https://worlddatabaseofhappiness.eur.nl/publications/using-the-past-to-enhance-the-present-boosting-happiness-through-positive-reminiscence-5467
16. **More often isn't always better, and variety slows adaptation.** Counting blessings once a week raised gratitude; three times a week did not. *Weak* (secondary accounts only). https://content.time.com/time/specials/2007/article/0,28804,1631176_1630611_1630512,00.html The Hedonic Adaptation Prevention model holds that variety and continued appreciation slow the fading of gains (3-wave longitudinal study, N=481). *Moderate.* https://sonjalyubomirsky.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Sheldon-Lyubomirsky-2012.pdf In an RCT, support for variety in an exercise programme raised adherence, mediated by perceived variety. *Moderate.* https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/variety-support-and-exercise-adherence-behavior-experimental-and-

**Well-being content**

17. **Positive-psychology intervention effects are small once small-sample bias is corrected** (r≈.10 for well-being). *Strong (methodological).* https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6541265/ · *Meridian:* no exercise should be sold as transformative.
18. **Behavioural activation (scheduling valued or pleasant activities) improved well-being in non-clinical people too** (g=0.52, 20 RCTs). *Moderate/strong.* https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20539837 · *Meridian:* the strongest evidence that a routine portfolio of valued activities is itself a well-being intervention.
19. **Days with more creative activity than usual were followed by higher flourishing; the reverse direction was not found** (13-day diary, N=658). *Moderate.* https://ourarchive.otago.ac.nz/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Everyday-creative-activity-as-a-path/9926515230401891
20. **Nature: at least 120 minutes a week was associated with good health and well-being,** whether in one visit or several. Benefits plateaued at 200–300 minutes. Cross-sectional. *Moderate.* https://neurosciencenews.com/nature-health-wellbeing-14233/ · *Meridian:* a natural weekly-total routine with an "about" target.
21. **A one-week playfulness intervention gave short-term well-being gains;** "use playfulness in a new way" worked best (N=533). *Moderate/weak.* https://zmescience.com/science/playfulness-training-experiment-82624423

**Relationships and social connection**

23. **Social integration is linked to survival** (OR 1.50 across 148 studies; 1.91 for complex measures of integration). *Strong (observational).* https://doaj.org/article/c9833ba608474b048444b92ae7ee48ad
24. **People underestimate how much others appreciate being contacted, especially when it's a surprise** (>5,900 participants, including field studies). *Strong.* https://www.psypost.org/2022/08/study-identifies-a-robust-tendency-to-underestimate-how-much-others-appreciate-being-reached-out-to-63627 · *Meridian:* this is the specific belief that stops people reaching out to friends and family.
25. **People mispredict that solitude will be nicer than talking to a stranger,** and weak-tie interactions correlate with daily happiness. *Moderate.* https://www.chicagobooth.edu/review/talk-to-a-stranger-itll-make-you-happier ; https://repository.essex.ac.uk/15300/
26. **Friendship takes roughly 50 hours to become casual and 200 or more to become close;** hours spent hanging out count more than hours working side by side. *Moderate* (figures vary between reports). https://news.ku.edu/2018/03/06/study-reveals-number-hours-it-takes-make-friend
27. **Novel or exciting shared activities.** In an RCT with 101 couples, 90 minutes a week of exciting activities for 4 weeks raised satisfaction, with gains still present at 4 months. *Moderate.* https://rune.une.edu.au/web/handle/1959.11/14155 Replications are mixed, and much of the positive evidence comes from one group. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11761567/
28. **Responding enthusiastically when a partner shares good news (capitalization) is linked to relationship well-being.** *Moderate* (correlational). https://www.sas.rochester.edu/psy/people/faculty/reis_harry/assets/pdf/GableReisImpettAsher_2004.pdf
29. **Rituals differ from routines by carrying symbolic meaning and being remembered warmly;** rigid routines can harm. *Moderate* (review). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12561283/ ; https://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/2002/12/rituals
30. **Tracking relationships ("the quantified relationship") is ethically contested:** it risks surveillance, commodification and a narrowed view of the relationship. The authors nonetheless see room for supportive uses. *Moderate (philosophical).* https://philarchive.org/rec/DANTQR

**Self-tracking practice**

31. **People lapse and resume tracking as a normal part of life;** the lived-informatics model treats lapses as expected. https://library.selfresearch.org/research/a-lived-informatics-model-of-personal-informatics/ ; https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~jfogarty/publications/ubicomp2016.pdf Devices are abandoned when they clash with self-image, when the data seems useless, or when upkeep is a burden. https://thatlab.umd.edu/papers/2015_p635-lazar.pdf *Moderate.*
32. **Bullet journalers build personal visualisations and treat tracking as a mindful, crafted practice.** *Moderate (qualitative).* https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10044078/ Long-term trackers turn old data into personal accounts of their past ("documentary informatics"). https://nrl.northumbria.ac.uk/id/eprint/34846
33. **Short reflective dialogues about one's own data prompted reflection and new behaviours;** 16 of 33 participants kept using the system unpaid. *Weak/moderate.* https://faculty.washington.edu/garyhs/docs/kocielnik-UBICOMP2018-reflection.pdf
34. **Perfectionism–tracking link: thin, cross-sectional.** *Weak.* https://czasopisma.bg.ug.edu.pl/index.php/CIiPP/article/view/10474
35. **Life transitions briefly loosen habits.** After moving house, an intervention worked only within roughly the first 3 months. *Weak/moderate.* https://pure.ulster.ac.uk/en/publications/cracks-in-the-wall-habit-discontinuities-as-vehicles-for-behaviou/

## 2. Reasonable hypotheses (inferences, not findings)

- **H-A: Routines differ in what makes them last.** Daily practices lean on context and repetition (4); low-frequency, leisure and relational routines lean on meaning, anticipation, memory and experienced satisfaction (1, 2, 5, 13–15, 29). Keep the data model generic (R-01) but vary the *feedback lens*.
- **H-B: A twice-monthly routine should feel like a rhythm kept alive and memories accumulating**, not a ratio filling. Anticipation, a keepsake and later rediscovery give three moments of value instead of one tick (14, 15, 32).
- **H-C: Counting suits instrumental routines but threatens intrinsically enjoyed ones** (12). For the latter, default to descriptive feedback (when, with whom, what was new), numbers on request.
- **H-D: Make lapses read as temporary and external** through visible context, built-in slack (9) and dignified repair (8). Structure likely beats kind wording alone (6, 7).
- **H-E: A portfolio of valued activities is itself an evidence-based intervention** (18), if shown as something to tend, not a balance score.

## 3. Speculative opportunities (worth prototyping)

1. **Loose "next time" intentions.** An optional, roughly timed intention on the routine itself ("date sometime this weekend"), not a calendar entry. Anticipation without chore-feel (13, 14); must not become a planner.
2. **Keepsake on logging.** An optional photo, word or place, never a rating (§0), resurfaced months later as rediscovery (15, 32). A results source that never runs out (R-20).
3. **Variety seeds.** Optional "something new this time?" (place, route, recipe) against hedonic adaptation (16, 21, 27).
4. **Reserves per period.** Spendable slack, so a miss reads "reserve used" (9).
5. **Non-destructive, repairable runs** counted in periods, pauses excluded, repaired via reserves or context: keeps the pull of continuity (8) without the cost of breakage.
6. **Reach-out moments.** Optional, Mark-controlled invitations for connection routines, never "overdue", informed by finding 24.
7. **Shared partner routines.** Both can see or confirm a date routine: shared ownership, not monitoring (30), plus a small capitalization channel (28). Consent design needed.
8. **Seasonal portfolio review.** A short ritual to keep, pause, retire or start routines, also at life transitions (35). Reflection, not reset.
9. **Short optional reflection dialogues** on what a routine has been giving him (33, 1), targeting experienced satisfaction.
10. **Mark-authored lenses.** He shapes how a routine's history is drawn, as bullet journalers do (32).

## 4. Challenges to current assumptions

- **"Avoid streaks" is too blunt.** Unbroken streaks *do* raise continuation (8). The harm comes from breakage that people attribute to themselves. Non-destructive and repairable continuity may beat having no continuity at all.
- **Habit-strength curves (H-03) don't transfer to weekly or monthly routines.** Repetition count didn't predict automaticity for weekly behaviour (5), and the daily-curve evidence is itself thin (4). A "consistency estimate" for a twice-monthly date risks false precision.
- **Implementation intentions (E-01) can backfire for leisure and relationships.** Precise time-and-place plans make leisure feel like work (13). Loose timing is the better default there.
- **"Results as reward" (Mark's own hypothesis) needs splitting by routine type.** For intrinsically enjoyed activities, quantified results can reduce enjoyment and continued engagement (12). Memory, novelty and anticipation may be the better "result".
- **Fresh starts:** offering a *new* goal after a break helps (8), but must not erase history (R-21).
- **More often isn't better by default** (16); never push higher cadence.
- **Packaged positive-psychology exercises are weak** (17); Mark's own valued activities (18) are the stronger lever.

## 5. Open questions

1. Which feedback lens (count, rhythm, memory, novelty) fits which routine, and can it be inferred from cadence and measure type?
2. Does a keepsake or rediscovery loop actually sustain a twice-monthly routine over a year, or is it only pleasant?
3. How do reserves and repair work across cadences without artificial mechanics (R-20)?
4. What consent model lets a partner share a routine without surveillance?
5. Can occasional, optional captures of experienced satisfaction feed maintenance without becoming a quality rating (§0)?
6. Which representations trigger Mark's all-or-nothing thinking? Needs prototypes tested with him, not literature.
