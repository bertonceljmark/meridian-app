# R5: Original engagement mechanisms, beyond what we've already discussed

Contract v1.1.1. This answers Mark's 13:28 request (owner-messages.md, point 6). Labels: **[E]** evidence I checked, **[H]** my inference, **[S]** speculation. "Unverified" means I found the source but couldn't read the figure myself.

## The blunt summary first

1. **Every concept so far was about (c), the reward afterwards.** Tempo, Forecast, Almanac, Loadout, Broadcast and Questions all act after the act is logged. Rhythm partly addressed (a). Nothing addressed (b), the activity itself. Of Mark's struggles, "starting beneficial activities" is the first he listed, and nothing previously targeted it. The strongest new mechanisms below work on (a) and (b).
2. **None of them is a universal engine.** None works for water, dates, observations and workouts alike. If Mark needs one mechanism that runs the whole product, nothing here beats what was already discussed. I think the search for a single universal engine is itself the mistake. A twice-monthly date doesn't need an engine; a walk he struggles to start does.
3. **A rule that settles "unrecorded vs missed":** pick mechanisms whose state depends only on positive events (a recorded start, a played episode, a recorded route). They never need to decide what a silent day meant, so the missing-data problem cannot arise. All four survivors pass this test, and Tempo does not.
4. **Do not combine the survivors.** They are separate bets for separate routine types, and each should be tested on its own.

## 2. Survivors

### A. Bundle: the thing you want, only while you're doing the thing you should

**Core loop.** Mark ties something he already craves to a routine he finds dull, for example a podcast series or audiobook to walking, or a show's audio to stretching. Meridian holds the next episode. It plays when a session is under way (a Watch workout is running or he starts a session) and pauses when the session ends, often mid-scene. The way back into the story is to start the routine again. Afterwards the session report notes where the story got to ("episode 7, three walks in"). Over months, history picks up natural landmarks with no input at all: "the walks of the *Dune* autumn".

**Evidence.**
- [E] Gym-only audiobooks raised gym visits by 51% at first, and an encouragement-only version by 29%. The effect faded, especially after a holiday break. Afterwards, 61% of participants chose to pay for gym-only access ([Milkman, Minson & Volpp 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25843979/), N = 226).
- A follow-up with more than 6,000 24 Hour Fitness members exists ([Kirgios 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.obhdp.2020.09.003)). I couldn't read its effect sizes (unverified).
- [E, weak] Zombies, Run! users say the story makes them run longer and keep coming back (30 interviews, [Farič 2021](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10139156/)).

**Why Mark.**
- [H] Years of social media and YouTube mean he has strong existing wants to borrow.
- ADHD delay discounting (r1·20) says the reward should come *during* the activity, and here it does.
- It asks nothing of his perfectionism: no numbers, nothing to rate.
- It sits beside his workout stats without replacing them.

**Hard cases.**
- *Date:* doesn't apply, and shouldn't. The date is already the want.
- *Monthly routine:* bundle an album or a film to the monthly long task.
- *Paused routine:* the bundle waits at the same place.
- *3-week absence:* the story is exactly where he left it. It isn't a debt; the cliffhanger is a pull (the resumption effect above).
- *Unrecorded vs missed:* it only knows what played during sessions and infers nothing.

**Zero extra input.** Choosing a series takes one tap per series. Starting a Watch workout releases the episode automatically.

**Serves** (a) and (b), plus (c) as landmarks.

**Failure modes.**
- The effect decays, which is the evidence's main warning.
- The routine dies when Mark tires of the series, and choosing the next one could become a chore.
- He may resent having his own pleasure locked. Encouragement alone was much weaker (29%), so the lock carries the effect.
- *Feasibility [S]:* Meridian could play open podcast RSS feeds itself. Locking other apps would need Screen Time's app shields, which I haven't verified for this purpose.
- *Safety:* release one episode per session so it never rewards ever-longer exercise (R-22).
- *Secretly a streak, journal or chore?* None of these.

**5-minute prototype.** Pick a series, start a simulated walk, hear audio, stop the walk mid-sentence, and see "resumes on your next walk". The question is whether the pause feels like a pull or like being policed. If it feels like policing, the idea is disproved. A prototype can't prove it works; that needs about 3 weeks on his own phone.

### B. Launch: starting is the move

**Core loop.** The unit Meridian credits is the smallest act Mark controls that starts the routine:
- starting a session on the Watch;
- tapping an NFC tag on the guitar case;
- for a social routine, *asking*.

Starting plays a short count-in he chose himself, like a musician's "1-2-3-4", as one haptic phrase that is the same every time. The start counts in full the moment it happens. A 4-minute walk is a complete Launch, and anything longer is extra that the session report shows. Starting takes about two seconds, with no setup screen, the way "Play" works in chess or "Battle" in Clash Royale. (Those are evidence about mechanism, not designs to copy.)

**Evidence.**
- [E] Rituals before consumption made it more enjoyable. Random gestures didn't, and watching someone else perform the ritual didn't either ([Vohs et al. 2013](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2013/07/130722072037.htm)).
- [E] Rituals improved self-control, but only when there was a self-control conflict ([Tian et al. 2018](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/jschroeder/Publications/Enacting%20Rituals%20to%20Improve%20Self-Control.pdf)).
- [E] Pre-performance routines improve sports performance (g ≈ 0.64 in experiments, [Rupprecht 2021](https://genderandagency.univie.ac.at/forschung/detailansicht/pure/88ac7734-a716-4c82-931d-cb38ec0b6124/show/publ/Pure)). That is performance, not initiation.
- [E] Once people start a task, they tend to resume and finish it (Ovsiankina, above).
- [E] Range goals ("2–4") improved re-engagement over single numbers ([Scott & Nowlis 2013](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jconrs/doi10.1086-670766.html)). The start is the bottom of the range.
- [S] No study tests count-ins for starting activities in ADHD.

**Why Mark.** This targets his first listed struggle. Crediting the start immediately suits delay discounting. Fully crediting a short start defuses the all-or-nothing thinking he names in himself, because there is no partial Launch.

**Hard cases.**
- *Date:* the Launch is the invitation, the part he controls; whether it happens depends on her too. Logging the date implies a Launch, so recording the invitation is optional.
- *Monthly routine:* works the same way.
- *Paused routine:* nothing is expected.
- *3-week absence:* the next start is just a start, with no "welcome back" copy (r4·6).
- *Unrecorded vs missed:* only starts exist, so it never infers that he didn't start.

**Zero extra input.** Watch and imported workouts count as Launches automatically. The count-in is optional.

**Serves** (a), with a little of (b).

**Failure modes.**
- The real bottleneck may come *before* the tap, getting up off the sofa. Launch only helps once the intention exists.
- Crediting starts could shorten sessions. Check in his data.
- The count-in may lose its effect the way HeartSteps prompts did. Rituals are performed by the person rather than delivered to them, which may protect them [H], but this is untested.
- A perfectionist might feel a 4-minute start is "cheating".
- *Streak?* No. *Chore?* Only if the ritual becomes mandatory, so it must never be required.

**5-minute prototype.** A Watch face with a long press, a count-in (visual and audio as a stand-in for haptics), a session running, a stop at 3 minutes, and full credit. The question is whether the start feels like an event and whether full credit for 3 minutes feels like relief or condescension. If Mark calls it patronising, the idea is disproved.

### C. Territory: new ground, from the real world

**Core loop.**
- Every outdoor session's route, which HealthKit already records ([route reading](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/reading-route-data.md)), is painted onto Mark's real map.
- New streets and paths are the reward.
- *Before:* a widget can show the nearest street he has never walked.
- *During:* the Watch gives one soft tap when he steps onto ground he has never covered, so exploring becomes the game of the walk.
- *After:* today's new segments ink in.
- Nothing ever fades back.

**Evidence.**
- [E, correlational] Days with more varied movement went with higher positive affect, including the following day (GPS, N = 122, [Heller 2020](https://www.hartleylab.org/uploads/5/3/1/0/53101939/heller%20_et_al._2020.pdf)).
- [E] CityStrides reports about 90,000 users, and some have completed their whole home town ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CityStrides)).
- [E] Pokémon GO added 955 steps a day in week 1, but the effect was back near baseline and non-significant by week 6 ([Howe 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5154977)). Location novelty moves people, and then wears off.

**Why Mark.**
- The world supplies novelty that never comes from Meridian's content team. That addresses "losing interest when novelty disappears".
- It fits his Strava map habit.
- *Travel becomes the richest case instead of a disrupted week* (AS-08).

**Hard cases.**
- *Date:* if he logs it on the spot with location permission, the place joins "places we've been" with no extra input. Otherwise it simply has no place.
- *Monthly routine:* a monthly hike adds a trail.
- *Paused routine and 3-week absence:* the map is unchanged.
- *Unrecorded vs missed:* walks without the Watch just don't appear, and the map never claims he stayed in.

**Zero extra input.** Yes, for recorded routes.

**Serves** (a), (b) and (c), but only for routines that happen in places.

**Failure modes.**
- **Completionism.** "Finish the neighbourhood" is exactly the trap for a perfectionist. A city percentage would make it a quota, so it should show no completion percentage by default.
- **Local saturation.** New streets near home run out within months. That conflicts with the rule that progression never depends on a finite collection, unless he travels.
- **Narrowness.** If it became the core, it would repeat the walking-only failure.
- **Privacy and safety.** It keeps a location history, and lures must never point him into unsafe areas or out at night.
- *Streak, journal or chore?* None of these.

**5-minute prototype.** Load Mark's *real* exported routes (from Strava or Health) onto a map. That's the decisive test; synthetic routes prove nothing. Show the new segments animating in and the widget's "unwalked street, 300 m". Ask him one question: would you go and walk that street tonight?

### D. Session shapes: every session has a recognisable form (weakest survivor)

**Core loop.**
- After a workout or a night's sleep, Meridian names its *shape* from the data, the way a chess game gets an opening name: "negative split", "steady zone 2", "two-part walk", "late and unbroken night".
- Over time Mark learns his repertoire of shapes, with no grading.
- Optionally, before a session, he can choose a shape to *try*. The Watch then gives quiet haptic guidance during the session, for example "drifting out of zone 2", and the report says whether the session took that shape.
- Shapes are lateral, never harder each time.

**Evidence.**
- [E] Feedback tied to success raised curiosity; amplification didn't (r4·1).
- [E] Supporting variety raised exercise adherence (r4·12).
- [E] Flow needs challenge matched to skill (r1·18).
- [E, against] Measurement can lower enjoyment (r1·5). Mark's explicit love of stats is the counterweight (13:28, point 4).

**Why Mark.** It gives the tactical micro-choice of chess and Clash Royale, *during* the activity, built from the stats he loves.

**Hard cases.**
- *Date:* must not apply (Etkin; §0 bans forced statistics).
- *Monthly routine:* a monthly long hike gets shapes.
- *Paused routine and absence:* nothing is owed.
- *Unrecorded vs missed:* shapes come only from recorded sessions.

**Zero extra input.** Yes, because naming the shape afterwards is automatic. Trying a shape is optional.

**Serves** (b) and (c).

**Failure modes.**
- It is the closest of the four to rejected ideas: a menu of shapes is Loadout's hand in disguise, and "first time you've done this shape" is records.
- A perfectionist may feel he *failed* a shape he tried.
- The set of shapes is finite unless they are generated from the data. That needs research.
- It covers workouts and sleep only.

**5-minute prototype.** Use three synthetic walks to show the shape each one was given. Then show a "try: steady zone 2" walk with haptic cues simulated, ending as "drifted at minute 18". If he reads the ending as a fail, the idea is disproved.

## 3. Ranking

1. **Bundle.** Best evidence for changing behaviour (a 51% initial effect) and it improves the activity itself. The main risk is decay.
2. **Launch.** Targets Mark's first-named struggle, with modest evidence and the least risk. It may be too small to feel like anything.
3. **Territory.** The most *enjoyable* on paper and the best at travel, but narrow and locally finite.
4. **Shapes.** Keep it only if Mark's reaction to the prototype is strong. Otherwise drop it.

For water and observations, none of these applies. Their satisfaction stays in the logging interaction itself, and that's fine.
