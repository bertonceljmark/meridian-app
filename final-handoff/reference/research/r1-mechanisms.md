# R1: Engagement mechanisms: what stays rewarding for years

Contract v1.1.1. Most figures come from abstracts or secondary summaries, which are marked (abs) or (sec).

## 1. Established evidence

1. **Enjoyment in the moment predicts persistence better than delayed payoff.** Immediate rewards predicted persistence in resolutions and gym sessions; delayed benefits did not (5 studies, sec). Moderate. https://www.chicagobooth.edu/review/2016/july/achieve-long-term-goals-focus-short-term · https://www.psypost.org/what-really-keeps-people-committed-to-long-term-goals-this-study-offers-a-key-insight/ → *The doing, and the seconds after it, must be the main reward. Later results come second.*
2. **Autonomous motivation predicts habit, both on its own and by strengthening the link between past behaviour and habit.** Cross-sectional, N=192. https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1349432/ SDT need satisfaction relates to health outcomes in 184 datasets (https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1745691612447309). SDT interventions have modest, heterogeneous effects (abs; https://pure.ewha.ac.kr/en/publications/a-meta-analysis-of-self-determination-theory-informed-interventio/). Strong for the association, weak for app-delivered effects. → *Self-endorsement is a structural requirement, not decoration.*
3. **Expected, contingent rewards undermine free-choice intrinsic motivation.** Engagement-contingent d = −0.40, completion-contingent d = −0.36. Positive feedback enhances it. Strong, though debated. https://home.ubalt.edu/ntygmitc/642/Articles%20syllabus/Deci%20Koestner%20Ryan%20meta%20IM%20psy%20bull%2099.pdf → *Acknowledgement should be informational ("this is what you did"), not a payment.*
4. **Negative feedback is much less demotivating when it gives concrete guidance and refers to objective rather than normative standards** (79 studies, abs). Moderate. https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/research/feedback → *Compare Mark with his own past and with the rule he chose, never with norms.*
5. **Measuring an enjoyable activity raises output but lowers enjoyment**; it makes the activity feel like work (6 experiments). Moderate. https://marketing.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Etkin-Jordan-11-12-15-Hidden-Cost.pdf → *Strong support for §0 "feedback fits the routine". Numbers can damage intrinsically enjoyable routines.*
6. **Points, levels and leaderboards raised task quantity but did not change competence or intrinsic motivation.** They acted as extrinsic incentives. Moderate. https://glassmanlab.seas.harvard.edu/annotated_works/mekler17.pdf → *Points push volume. That is a safety risk for water, exercise and caffeine (R-22).*
7. **Gamification effects are small and context-dependent, and they fade.** Behavioural g = .25, and the effect is unstable in rigorous studies (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w). Perceived benefit declines with tenure (cross-sectional; https://researchportal.tuni.fi/en/publications/demographic-differences-in-perceived-benefits-from-gamification/). Hamari et al. name novelty effects as a recurring caveat (https://www.lescahiersdelinnovation.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/2014-hamari_et_al-does_gamification_work.pdf). Strong.
8. **People speed up as a goal approaches and slump after reaching it.** Illusory progress also accelerates effort. Strong. https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/1200/goalgradient.pdf → *Finite goals create cliffs after completion. Rolling or regenerating horizons avoid the slump.*
9. **A single metric comes to replace the goal it stood for ("surrogation").** Two things reduce this: using several measures, and letting the person choose the strategy themselves. Taking part in deliberation alone does not help. Moderate (accounting experiments). https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/facpub/8208 · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrogation → *This supports authorship and several facets per routine.*
10. **Information is a reward in its own right.** Monkeys prefer outcome-irrelevant advance information, encoded by dopamine neurons (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/2723053). Curiosity is a felt gap that needs some prior knowledge (https://stafforini.com/works/loewenstein-1994-psychology-curiosity-review/). An uncertain reward size motivates more effort than a certain one, when people focus on the process (sec; https://www.chicagobooth.edu/review/uncertainty-can-improve-motivation). Strong for information-seeking, moderate for uncertainty.
11. **Checking habits form from very fast informational payoffs** and feel like an annoyance (https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2011/07/110725101222.htm). **People avoid information when they expect bad news:** retirement-account logins fall 9.5% after market declines (https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/FinancialAttention.pdf). Strong. → *Mark's morning score check fits information-seeking. Expect him to avoid the app after bad stretches. Design so that bad news is safe to see.*
12. **Social media posting follows reward-learning dynamics driven by likes** (>1M posts; https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/a-computational-reward-learning-account-of-social-media-engagemen/, abs). Loot-box spending is linked to problem gambling, η² = .054 (https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/139116/). Strong. → *Randomised reward drops are excluded on safety grounds.*
13. **Being paid to return after a missed workout was the top performer of 54 interventions** (61k gym members). Only about 8% of effects lasted after the programmes ended (sec; https://www.scimex.org/newsfeed/which-programs-give-gym-goers-the-biggest-boost). **Flexible incentives beat routine-time incentives**, both during and after the intervention (https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v67y2021i7p4139-4171.html). Strong. → *Recovery should be an active, positive event, not only an absence of punishment. Don't impose fixed times.*
14. **Variety slows hedonic adaptation** (481 students; https://sonjalyubomirsky.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Sheldon-Lyubomirsky-2012.pdf). But in app data from 218,606 users, **a wider variety of activities was associated with *smaller* well-being gains**, because varied users chose less effective activities (https://doi.org/10.1080/17439760.2021.2006760). Moderate. → *Novelty belongs in perspectives and feedback, not in churning the behaviours.*
15. **Choice increases intrinsic motivation and perceived competence.** The effect is larger with 2–4 choices and when no reward follows, and smaller in adults. Strong. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18298272/
16. **Self-made things are overvalued (IKEA effect), but only when completed.** Building and then destroying, or leaving unfinished, removes the effect. Strong. https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=1777100 Bullet journalers build their own trackers and visualisations as mindful practice (https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10044078/). Moderate.
17. **Positive fantasies about the ideal future sap energy** (https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/46284). **Developers using AI took 19% longer but believed they were 20% faster** (n=16; https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/). Moderate. → *Authoring and imagining a system can feel like progress without being progress.*
18. **Flow depends on challenge–skill balance, clear goals and a sense of control** (28 studies; https://www.instituteofcoaching.org/resources/challenge%E2%80%93skill-balance-and-antecedents-flow-meta-analytic-investigation). Game enjoyment tracks need satisfaction, with clear, consistent feedback named as a key feature (https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/player-experience-of-needs-satisfaction-pens/). Moderate–strong.
19. **Identity labels don't work, but value-linked habits do.** Noun-identity priming ("be a voter") failed large preregistered replications (https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/behavioural-public-policy/article/abs/voting-behavior-is-unaffected-by-subtle-linguistic-cues-evidence-from-a-psychologically-authentic-replication/7CA9BE69C3213D5030A2BD11A5C53450). Links between habit and true self strengthen when the behaviour is tied to the values it serves (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6635880/). Reading difficulty as a sign of importance helps, versus reading it as impossibility (mostly one lab; https://dornsife.usc.edu/daphna-oyserman/identity/). Moderate.
20. **ADHD:** steeper delay discounting, d = 0.43 (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5049699). Reinforcement improves performance and motivation, and immediate rewards are preferred (children; abs; https://ulibertadores.academia.edu/ADSHD). The **"interest-based nervous system" has no peer-reviewed test**; it is a clinician's framework (https://lifeskillsadvocate.com/blog/interest-based-nervous-system/). → *Immediacy matters more for Mark. Don't build on the "interest-based nervous system" claim.*
21. **Dark patterns include grinding and "appointment play"** (https://research.chalmers.se/en/publication/177148). Trackers routinely lapse and resume (https://library.selfresearch.org/research/a-lived-informatics-model-of-personal-informatics/). Strong.

## 2. Reasonable hypotheses (inferences)

**How Mark's products map to mechanisms.** He reported each product. The mechanisms below are inferred, and so is what transfers.

| Product | Reported | Plausible mechanism | What transfers without the form |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chess | months | high skill ceiling; every game a new problem; rating as *informational* competence feedback; reviewing one's own game | competence feedback where a real skill exists; reviewing one's own record |
| Clash Royale | months | short loops resolved by an outcome; composing a strategy (the deck) that reality then tests; unlocks; random chests (the harmful part) | composing an approach and seeing it tested |
| Social media | years | social reward learning; novelty; near-zero effort | relatedness through real people; low effort; *not* feeds |
| Claude building | intense now | autonomy + competence + immediate visible results; IKEA effect; endless headroom | authorship of one's own system, with visible consequences |
| Sleep Cycle | intermittent | an effortless morning reveal: resolving uncertainty about oneself | genuine reveals about oneself |
| Strava | enjoys | a rich after-action report; PRs as self-comparison; kudos (social reward, sec: https://runningmagazine.ca/?p=91520) | a rich account of a session; comparison with oneself |

- **H1. The common thread is not "results". It is consequential agency with fast, informative resolution:** I chose something, reality answered, and I learned something about my system or myself. Chess, Clash and Claude all have it. Sleep Cycle and Strava provide the resolution half.
- **H2. Most routines have low skill ceilings** (water, medication, a date). For those, the mastery lies **elsewhere**:
  - in *knowing oneself* (predicting and understanding one's patterns);
  - in *designing one's own system*;
  - for skill-rich routines (training, music, cooking), in the craft itself. Deterding's "intrinsic skill atoms" says to find the challenge already inside the activity (https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/120651/).
- **H3. Mark's enjoyment of results "even when I struggle to perform" suggests the results reward *inspecting*, not *doing*.** A results loop could therefore keep the app opened without keeping routines going (findings 11, 17).
- **H4. Relational and rare routines are rewarded by anticipation and memory, not metrics.** Savoring before and reminiscence after (https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/AnticipationDelayed.pdf).

## 3. Speculative opportunities (worth prototyping)

1. **Self-calibration as a mastery domain.** Before a reveal (a night, a workout, a week), Mark can optionally give a one-tap guess, and reality answers. Over months, Meridian shows how well he knows himself. An endless, honest skill built on real information gaps (finding 10), with no randomised rewards. Risks are chore-like effort and self-surveillance, so it must be optional, sparse and never scored as failure.
2. **Authorship with consequences, plus a cooling period.** Mark writes and revises his own rules (cadence, facets, what counts) as versions. Each version must be lived with for a while before its effect is shown as "how v3 played out against v2". This captures the building loop and reduces surrogation (finding 9), with a brake on tinkering (finding 17).
3. **The comeback as a first-class event.** Returning after a lapse earns a distinct, warm acknowledgement and an easy re-entry path. This builds on the megastudy result (finding 13) without paying for lapses. Guard against it becoming a reason to lapse.
4. **Novelty in lenses, not behaviours.** The same history reveals new perspectives over time (new comparisons, periods of his life, rare coincidences), unlocked by data maturity, not grind. This counters adaptation (finding 14) without fragmenting routines.
5. **Anticipation and memory for low-frequency routines.** An upcoming date or trip is savoured: visible, approaching, and not a task. Afterwards an optional one-line memory accrues into something beautiful. No rating (§0).
6. **An intent line beside every metric.** Each routine shows *why* in Mark's own words next to its numbers (findings 9 and 19). A value affirmation, not a label.
7. **A craft layer only where there is skill.** Technique notes, difficulty and progressions are opt-in per routine. Chess-like depth goes where it exists and is never forced (§0).
8. **Safe bad news.** Reveals during bad stretches lead with context and what is still intact, to counter the ostrich effect (finding 11). They never hide the truth.

## 4. Challenges to current assumptions

- **"Results as reward" (Mark's hypothesis) is weakly supported as a driver of *behaviour*.**
  - Measurement can reduce enjoyment (finding 5), and fast payoffs build checking habits (finding 11).
  - Mark's own report separates enjoying results from doing.
  - Results should depend on the doing, and the moment of doing must carry its own reward (finding 1).
- **Novelty and variety, part of Mark's explanation, can backfire** when applied to behaviours (finding 14).
- **Streak avoidance is necessary but passive.** The evidence favours *actively rewarding the return* (finding 13).
- **Records, levels and points increase volume, not motivation** (finding 6). That conflicts with the "never encourage excess" rule for several routines.
- **Fresh starts, seasons and finite goals bring post-goal slumps** (finding 8). Bounded horizons need to regenerate.
- **Habit-strength curves:** autonomy, not repetition alone, predicts habit (finding 2). Reminders and cues address repetition, not ownership.
- **Identity framing ("become someone who…") as copy is unsupported** (finding 19). Accumulated evidence linked to values is better supported.
- **Building may not transfer.** Building Meridian may itself be the game he enjoys. Inside it, authorship risks becoming "another demanding project to maintain" (his own warning, owner-messages Part I §2).
- **ADHD claims:** the "interest-based nervous system" lacks evidence. Delay discounting is solid and argues for immediacy, not novelty-chasing.

## 5. Open questions that matter most

1. Does inspecting results predict *subsequent doing*, or substitute for it? Testable in Mark's own data within weeks.
2. Where is Mark's mastery: craft, self-knowledge or system design? Offer all three in a concept test and see which he returns to.
3. How much authorship gives ownership without endless tinkering, and what cooling period feels respectful rather than restrictive?
4. What counts as a "reveal" for a routine with no measurable outcome, such as a date or a phone call?
5. How can bad news be safe enough that Mark keeps looking after a rough month?
6. Can self-calibration stay fun without becoming self-surveillance, especially for a perfectionist?
